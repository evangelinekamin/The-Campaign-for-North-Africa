## What 56.3 actually is

Not lighters, not barges, not a naval-transport allocation off the 56.4/56.5 convoy pool. It is a SMALL,
FIXED, Axis-only fleet of named counters ("several ship counters", 56.31) that shuttle ALREADY-LANDED
cargo along the coast, port to port — the general rule's own words: "a limited amount of coastal
shipping which he may use to transfer small amounts of supplies from one coastal port to another." It
is downstream of the overseas convoy (56.1/56.2), not a substitute for it: cargo must already be sitting
in a port dump (56.28) before a coastal ship can carry it onward.

## Cases (56.31-56.35), transcribed verbatim, page 75-76

> [56.31] Axis coastal shipping is represented by several ship counters. Each ship counter has its
> tonnage capacity printed on it. Each ship has a CPA — for movement only — of 50. Each sea hex costs
> one point.
>
> [56.32] Axis coastal shipping moves in the Truck Convoy Phase, and only in that Phase.
>
> [56.33] Axis coastal ships cannot be attacked from air or land. They may not enter a port that has
> been neutralized (Capacity Level of zero). They may also never enter an Enemy-occupied port.
>
> [56.34] To use coastal shipping, the Axis Player loads supplies (no personnel) on his boats at the
> beginning of the Truck Convoy Phase (at a cost of 5 CPs) and then moves the boat. Unloading supplies
> costs 5 CPs. Only one type of cargo may be carried at any one time by any one ship. Tanks, guns, etc.,
> may not be shipped via coastal shipping.
>
> [56.35] Coastal ships may stop and unload in one port and then continue on to another port, etc., as
> long as they do not exceed their CPA. Coastal ships do not require fuel.

No die is ever rolled anywhere in 56.31-56.35 — coastal shipping is fully deterministic (unlike the
overseas convoy's 56.21 tonnage die and 41.66 interdiction die). Confirmed against the scan: 56.33's
"cannot be attacked from air or land" is echoed verbatim at [41.34] ("Axis coastal shipping may not be
bombed") and [58.1] ("Note that the Axis coastal shipping ... may not be attacked"), so the immunity is
not a one-off.

## What this engine has no concept of, and how the build routes around it (named, not invented)

**There is no `Terrain.SEA` hex layer and no sea-hex adjacency graph anywhere in `game.terrain` /
`game.movement`.** `game.scenario`'s own standing convention is the opposite: "a hex a piece stands on
is land — coastal ports ... colour-sample as sea, so add every occupied hex as coastal CLEAR." 56.31's
"each sea hex costs one point" therefore cannot be walked hex-by-hex the way a land unit's CPA is
(`supply.reachable_truck_moves`, ZOC-aware pathing) — that data was never extracted, and Phase 8's own
terrain work (`cna-phase8-terrain-verdict` memory) traced LAND hexsides, not the Mediterranean.

**Separately, and independently: Tripoli — the fleet's printed home port (56.35/60.35) — has NO hex in
this engine's map sections A-E at all** (`game.scenario` line ~902: "The Axis Tripoli box ... is
off-map"). The full `campaign()` build seeds exactly TWO Axis-side `Port` objects with a real hex:
`PORT-Benghazi` (the forward Mediterranean harbour the overseas convoy actually lands at in this
engine) and `PORT-Tobruk`. There is no `PORT-Tripoli` in `_campaign_ports` at all — Tripoli's stock is
carried by an off-corridor abstraction (`_axis_rear`/`AX-Tripoli`-style dumps in the smaller Desert Fox
scenario only), not by a throttled, hex-anchored `Port`.

**Routing decision, following the precedent set by `[8.44]`/`5.33` and `22.3`/`21.6` (name the gap,
build the rest faithfully around it, do not invent the missing layer):**

1. Coastal shipping is built as a real, testable, event-sourced subsystem (`game.state.CoastalShip`,
   three new `EventKind`s, `game.engine._coastal_shipping`) with every CPA/CP magnitude above wired
   verbatim (50 / 1 / 5 / 5), the single-cargo-type constraint, the immunity from attack (honoured
   STRUCTURALLY — nothing in the combat/bombing code is ever given a `CoastalShip` to target, the same
   way `NavalUnit` and `AirWing` already are), and the neutralized-port / enemy-port entry gate.
2. For "sea hexes crossed" between two `Port`s, the engine uses `game.hexmap.distance` — the SAME
   straight-line axial hex-distance primitive already used elsewhere in this codebase as a geographic
   proxy where no traced route exists (`game.relay`'s truck-hop distances, `game.policy`'s and
   `game.campaign_policy`'s movement heuristics, and the rulebook's OWN [56.18] Air Distance Chart,
   which likewise ranges bases against a Rally Point by straight distance rather than a flown route).
   This is a PROXY, flagged here exactly as `data/wells.json`'s pipeline proxy is: it invents no
   terrain and crosses no land the ships could not swim over, it just does not trace the coastline's
   actual bends, so it is very likely a mild UNDER-estimate of the true 56.31 sea-hex cost between two
   distant ports. FLAGGED, not silently assumed.

   **CORRECTED 2026-07-31 — on the lane this campaign actually sails it is NOT a proxy at all, and
   the "mild under-estimate" hedge above was wrong.** The `[37.42]` LAND DISTANCE CHART (PDF p.072,
   read at 150dpi while locating 54.4) prints **Benghazi (A4827) to Tobruk (C4807) = 46 hexes**,
   which is exactly what `game.hexmap.distance` returns for that pair — and the chart's own note
   states its distances are measured "cutting across the Mediterranean (assuming an all-sea hex
   grid) where necessary." So the BOOK measures a sea passage as the straight chord too and does not
   bend it around the Cyrenaican bulge; the engine reproduces the book's own number. (Contrast
   `[56.26]`'s ROAD DISTANCE TABLE on PDF p.071, which prints the same pair as **102** by road —
   the 46-vs-102 split is precisely the advantage 56.3 is meant to confer.) The hedge stands only
   for some other port pair with a genuinely concave coast, not for Benghazi–Tobruk.
3. In the `campaign()` build only, Tripoli's ENGINE ROLE — the fleet's home port and the Axis's
   overseas-supply reservoir — is already played by `PORT-Benghazi` (see `_campaign_ports`'s own
   docstring: "the forward Mediterranean harbour the convoys land at"). The four ships are seeded there
   rather than at an invented on-map Tripoli hex, under the SAME kind of proxy note `scenario.py`
   already carries elsewhere ("the scenario proxy for off-corridor Tripoli/Alexandria," line ~507). The
   lane this therefore actually exercises is Benghazi \<-\> Tobruk — historically also the single most
   consequential coastal-shipping run in the real campaign (Tripoli-Benghazi coastal traffic mattered
   far less once the front sat east of Benghazi for most of the war).
4. The benchmark scenarios (`rommels_arrival`, `siege_of_tobruk`) seed NO ships — `GameState.ships`
   defaults to `()`, so both stay byte-identical, the same pattern `NavalUnit` already set (
   `tests/test_naval.py::test_scenarios_naval_silent_and_byte_identical`).
5. **Multi-stop voyages within one Phase (56.35's "stop and unload in one port and then continue on to
   another") are NOT built** — a ship completes at most one load-and-depart (or unload-and-return)
   decision per Truck Convoy Phase; a voyage that outruns 50 CP in one Phase resumes automatically next
   Phase (itself faithful — 56.32 gates WHEN a ship may move, not that a whole voyage must complete in
   one Phase). This is a scope simplification, not a rule violation: nothing in 56.35 REQUIRES chaining
   two ports in one Phase, it only PERMITS it when the CPA allows, and Benghazi\<-\>Tobruk (measured
   `game.hexmap.distance` 46) leaves no spare CPA for a same-Phase third leg regardless.

## MEASURED (5 seeds, 111-turn campaign: 1941, 7, 4, 24, 2026)

The fleet is intensely active under `coastal_shipping_doctrine`: 452-678 completed voyages per
campaign (roughly one round trip per Game-Turn per ship), 429-498 load events, 425-498 unload
events, 94-232 rejected orders (mostly a temporarily-neutralized Tobruk quay, or two ships
contending for the same dump's stock in one Phase — both expected, not bugs).

**Gross tons moved is large (531,000-621,000 t) but is mostly a WASH, not delivery.** The lane
counts split almost exactly 1:1 (e.g. seed 1941: Benghazi→Tobruk 229, Tobruk→Benghazi 233) because
the doctrine's "return empty to the richer port" leg round-trips every delivery — every arrival
at Tobruk is followed by a return voyage, and the doctrine re-evaluates "richest" fresh every
Phase, so it oscillates rather than settling. **NET forward delivery** (Tobruk-bound minus
Benghazi-bound unloads, per commodity) is far smaller and varies a lot by seed — Fuel alone: 4,000
t (seed 1941), 17,000 t (seed 7), 119,000 t (seed 4). Against the faucet's own ~1.9 million Fuel
Points (~237,500 t) landed over a campaign, that is genuinely a "small amount," matching 56.0's own
framing, even though the gross throughput number looks enormous.

**THE DECISIVE TEST: an A/B (same seed, `ships=()` vs the seeded fleet) shows the Axis's truck
last-mile tonnage delivered is BYTE-IDENTICAL, to the ton, in every one of 3 seeds tested** (1941:
27,949 t both ways; 7: 28,543 t both ways; 4: 20,923 t both ways). **56.3 relieves NOTHING of the
[53.0] truck bottleneck this project's faucet finding turns on — it is a pure ADDITION, running on
its own separate Benghazi/Tobruk larder, and the 2nd/3rd-line lorry park hauls exactly as much with
the fleet sailing as without it.** The two systems never interact: `CampaignAxisPolicy.truck_orders`
/ the relay chain has no knowledge of coastal shipping's cargo and would haul the same freight
regardless.

Winner and Victory Points are unchanged in every seed (still Axis Smashing Victory, same VP
totals) — consistent with the standing finding (`[[cna-the-faucet]]`) that no single Tier-2 item
alone moves the campaign's balance.

**The oscillation is a DOCTRINE-QUALITY property, not a rule fidelity gap.** 56.3 itself is built
and measured faithfully; `coastal_shipping_doctrine`'s naive "ship to whichever the richer port,
return empty otherwise, re-decided every Phase" heuristic is exactly the kind of "opinion a
commander may hold" the convoy-split precedent (`convoy_plan_doctrine`) already established a
pattern for, and it is flagged, not silently assumed optimal. A smarter doctrine (a deadband, or a
standing one-way relay instead of a re-decided shuttle) would likely raise the net-forward share —
that is tuning, and belongs at Gate D, not here.

## REVIEW REPAIR (2026-07-31) — the harbour throttle, the Phase head, and who eats the cargo

The adversarial review re-read PDF pp.75-76 and p.66 at 300dpi and confirmed the transcription above
is verbatim-correct with every printed magnitude wired at its printed value — but found the build
**bypassed the one constraint the book puts on coastal shipping**, plus three smaller defects. All
repaired:

1. **[55.3]/[56.27] THE HARBOUR THROTTLE WAS NOT APPLIED AT ALL.** The 55.3 chart legend makes a
   port's Maximum Tonnage ONE number covering everything shipped "in and/or out in one Operations
   Stage" (repeated by 55.13 "enter and leave", 55.14 "brought into or out of", 55.16 "all shipments
   received"), 56.27 says coastal cargo may not exceed it, and 48 V.C.7 says coastal shipping "is
   limited only by the port capacities". The build charged the quay nothing: **seed 1941 pushed
   349,790 points through Benghazi against a 2,500-ton per-stage budget.** The overseas convoy
   landing and 56.3 now draw on the SAME per-(turn, stage) ledger — convoy first, ships after, both
   at load AND unload. Post-fix Benghazi moves 47,482 with `peak_coastal=2500, over=0` and 319
   orders rejecting as capacity-spent.
2. **[56.34] ordering.** Loading happens "at the beginning of the Truck Convoy Phase", so
   `_coastal_shipping` now runs AHEAD of `_truck_convoys` — the ships, not the lorries, get first
   call on a quay's dump and its tonnage budget.
3. **Axis-only.** 48 V.C.7: "Allied coastal shipping is not represented by counters." The
   Commonwealth's side of the idea lives in its 57.0 abstraction, not a fleet.
4. **[56.33]/[56.15] a destination lost at sea.** Previously the ship loitered forever and its
   cargo left the game permanently; it now puts about for the port it sailed from, paying the sea
   hexes back at 56.31's one-point-per-hex. FLAGGED as a judgement call — the book has no at-sea
   state, because in 56.35 a voyage completes inside one Phase whenever the CPA covers it; 56.15
   (cancel a sailing whose destination the Enemy has taken) is the closest thing it prints.
5. **`CampaignStaffPolicy` inherited an empty `coastal_shipping_orders`** — the watchable-campaign
   path left the whole fleet tied at Benghazi for 111 turns while its scripted twin sailed it.
6. **Water is shippable.** 56.34 bars only personnel and "Tanks, guns, etc."; `CONVOY_COMMODITIES`
   belongs to [56.22]'s run from EUROPE, not a transfer between two African ports. The doctrine
   still ships only the three — the Axis draws water from wells — but that is now doctrine, not rule.

### WHY 56.3 IS INERT — MEASURED, and it is NOT only the faucet

The A/B stands and is now better understood. Axis truck last-mile tonnage is byte-identical with the
fleet sailing and with `ships=()` (17,972 t both ways on seed 1941; 15,773 on seed 4), signatures
differ (so the neuter genuinely reaches), winner and VP unchanged. The mechanism, measured on seed
1941, is **two independent and individually sufficient causes**:

* **The source never runs dry.** Over 355 truck draws at Benghazi across the whole war, the dump's
  low-water marks were ammo 97 / fuel 801 / stores 766. Removing cargo starves nobody. (This is the
  faucet, as expected.)
* **THE DESTINATION HAS NO CUSTOMER.** Of 28,779 points loaded, **18,741 (65%) are unloaded straight
  back into the dump they came from** — the doctrine's re-decided-every-Phase shuttle oscillating.
  The 8,190 that go forward land in `AX-Stage-Tobruk`, and **Axis trucks load from that dump ZERO
  times in 111 turns** (`AX-Benghazi` 355, `A4728-El Berca-Supply` 21, `AX-Stage-W1` 3,
  `AX-Stage-Bardia` 1).

**NAMED DEBT, not repaired here:** the Axis truck relay never stages out of Tobruk, so 56.3's forward
delivery feeds nothing. This is the exact analogue of 22.3, whose value turned out to be blocked by
the unbuilt 21.6 towing rather than by the rule. It is a DOCTRINE question (Gate C / Gate D), and no
relay heuristic was invented here to make the number move.
